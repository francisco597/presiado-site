What Is Level 5 Drywall Finishing — And Why It Matters in Sarasota

The construction industry rates drywall finish on a six-tier scale from Level 0 (unfinished board) to Level 5 (the highest standard). Level 4 — where joints, fasteners, and beads are taped, coated, and sanded smooth — is the standard trade finish. It is appropriate for matte and flat paint under normal residential lighting, and it is where most drywall scopes end.

Level 5 begins where Level 4 stops. A full skim coat of joint compound is applied across the entire wall surface, then sanded to a single uniform plane. The purpose is to eliminate the subtle differences between the taped joint areas and the field of the board — differences that are invisible under flat paint and ambient light, but become highly visible under raking light, critical lighting, or any sheen above eggshell.

In Sarasota’s premium homes, Level 5 is not a luxury upgrade. It is a baseline requirement — for great rooms with floor-to-ceiling glass, for entry foyers with directional lighting, for any wall that will receive semi-gloss or high-gloss paint, and for the kind of custom interiors where the architect specified one continuous surface, not a wall with visible joints.

Most drywall trades do not offer Level 5 because the trade standard ends at Level 4 and the skill required to skim-coat consistently is uncommon. Most painting providers do not offer it because drywall is not their trade. Presiado does both, and we elevate the finish standard for the homeowners who can tell the difference between a wall that was finished and a wall that was completed.
