What Level 5 Drywall Finishing Means

Level 5 is the highest standard of drywall finishing, designed to reduce visible seams, texture differences, flashing, and surface imperfections in areas where light or paint sheen reveals every detail.

A Level 5 finish includes an additional skim coat applied across the entire wall surface — not only at seams and fasteners — producing a continuous, paint-ready substrate that performs better under critical lighting and higher-sheen finishes.

When Level 5 is typically recommended:

  • Walls with strong natural or accent lighting
  • Smooth-finish interiors (no orange peel or knockdown)
  • Darker paint colors or high-pigment finishes
  • Gloss, semi-gloss, or satin sheens
  • Premium living areas, kitchens, and entries
  • Bathrooms, hallways, or accent walls where imperfections are visible

Why Drywall Finishing Matters Before Painting

Paint does not hide bad drywall. In many cases, it makes drywall problems more visible.

Seams, uneven texture, patch marks, flashing, and surface defects often appear only after primer or finish paint is applied. By that point, fixing the wall requires sanding, re-floating, and repainting — three jobs instead of one.

Presiado inspects the wall condition, repairs the surface, prepares the finish, and paints with the final result in mind. One scope, one company, one clean finish.

Our Documented Finish Process

Every drywall finishing project follows the same structured process — written, communicated, and walked through with the homeowner from the first site visit to the final inspection.

1

Site visit and surface review

Wall condition, lighting, sheen, and finish goal assessed in person.

2

Written scope

Exact finish level, materials, timeline, and price documented before any work begins.

3

Surface repair and prep

Patches, seams, fastener heads, and joint treatment completed to specification.

4

Skim coat application

Full-surface skim or targeted finishing per the agreed Level 4 or Level 5 standard.

5

Sanding and surface preparation

Progressive sanding for a uniform, paint-ready substrate.

6

Primer and paint readiness check

Surface inspected under appropriate lighting before priming begins.

7

Final walkthrough and documentation

Completion checklist signed off, materials and colors documented for future reference.

You May Need Drywall Finishing Before Painting If…

  • Seams or patches are visible on your existing walls
  • Natural light reveals defects after a previous paint job
  • You are planning a repaint with darker or higher-sheen colors
  • A previous repair left visible texture differences
  • A bathroom, hallway, or accent wall needs a cleaner finish
  • New construction or addition walls require finish-level prep before paint
