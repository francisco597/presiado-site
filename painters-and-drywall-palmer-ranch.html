THE UNIFIED-SYSTEM ANGLE

Why Most Sarasota Homeowners End Up Hiring Two Contractors — And Why You Don’t Have To

The standard pattern in Sarasota goes like this: a homeowner hires a drywall contractor to repair walls, then hires a separate painter to finish them. Two estimates. Two schedules. Two warranties. And when something goes wrong at the seam between the two trades — a visible patch under the paint, a texture that doesn’t match, a hairline that opens up after the first humid season — the painter blames the drywaller, the drywaller blames the painter, and the homeowner is left coordinating a fix neither one wants to own.

The reason this happens is structural. A drywall contractor’s scope ends at a Level 4 finish. A painter’s scope begins at primer. The space between those two definitions — the prep, the skim coat, the texture match, the spot priming — is where premium finishes live or die. When two contractors split that gap, the gap is what shows on your wall.

We execute drywall and painting as one phased project with a single proposal, a single timeline, and a single point of accountability. Phase one is surface assessment and drywall repair to a Level 4 or Level 5 finish, depending on the wall’s role in the room. Phase two is primer and finish coats, sequenced so the home stays functional. Phase three is an integrated walkthrough — not a drywall sign-off and a separate painting sign-off, but one inspection that closes the project.

What you get is what most Palmer Ranch homeowners actually wanted in the first place: a wall that looks the way the architect intended, finished by people who took responsibility for both the substrate and the surface. No finger-pointing. No coordination tax. One responsible owner from the first patch to the final coat.
