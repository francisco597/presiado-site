THE PROBLEM WITH HIRING TWO TRADES

Why Drywall and Paint Should Be One Project, Not Two

Most homeowners start with a paint refresh in mind, then discover the walls also need patching, ceiling repair, water damage remediation, or popcorn removal. The instinct is to call a drywaller and a painter separately. That instinct creates three problems.

Problem 1 — Accountability disappears. When the paint shows a halo around the patch six months later, the painter blames the drywaller’s prep, the drywaller blames the painter’s primer, and you pay to fix it. With one company on both phases, there is no one to blame but us — which means there is no one to fix it but us.

Problem 2 — The schedule doubles. Two trades coordinating means waiting for the drywaller to finish before the painter starts, then waiting again if the painter finds something the drywaller missed. One crew sequencing both phases is faster — sometimes by a week or more on a typical scope.

Problem 3 — You pay for prep and protection twice. Both trades quote setup, surface protection, and cleanup — and they both bill you. One company doing both quotes that work once. The savings are not theoretical: itemized side-by-side, the integrated proposal almost always comes in cleaner.

Presiado Home Improvement was built around this exact integration. Drywall finishing is a craft, painting is a craft, and the homeowners who care about the result deserve a company that takes both crafts seriously enough to deliver them as one project.

THE INTEGRATED PROCESS

Four Phases, One Crew, One Timeline

Every drywall + painting project follows the same disciplined sequence. No guesswork, no handoffs, no surprise back-charges.

1

Drywall Repair

Patches, water damage drywall replacement, ceiling repair, popcorn removal — whatever the scope requires, executed cleanly with surface protection in place from day one.

2

Texture Match

Match the surrounding texture (Knockdown, Orange Peel, or Smooth/Level 5) by hand. Test on a small area, confirm the match, then apply across the patch so it integrates invisibly.

3

Primer

Sand to a uniform surface, then prime the patched and surrounding area to unify sheen and seal the new drywall. This is where most two-trade projects fail — we don’t skip it.

4

Finish Coats

Sherwin-Williams or Benjamin Moore finish coats applied with the same care that goes into the drywall phase. Final inspection walkthrough closes both scopes together.

One proposal. One crew. One timeline. One accountable owner from first patch to final coat.
